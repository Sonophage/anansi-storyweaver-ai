# Talk does what it is asked, and chalks what it offers

Seth decided this on 2026-10-28, in the grill on Talk. The words are GLOSSARY.md's.

## Why

Talk was built to change nothing in the tale: its reply came back, the threads it suggested were
chalked, and a direction went to the composer unsent. A writer who asked "make Talia older" then had
to find the chalked change on the Workbench and sew it in, which is a second step for a thing they
had already said.

## The decision

**Asked means done.** What the writer asks Anansi in so many words to change in Talk, he changes at
once: the tale's thread (premise, setting, rules), any thread (Always true, Over time, pockets,
Always on or On a word, retired), and a scene's title and where it heads. Each change is shown in
the conversation with Undo, and is kept in the thread's history like any other edit, so it can be
put back later. A request is the writer asking, so it meets the rule that Anansi writes a thread's
Always true only when asked.

**Retire, never delete.** Asked to get rid of a thread in a saved tale, Talk retires it. Deleting
stays a press the writer makes by hand.

**Offered means chalked.** What Anansi suggests without being asked goes to the Workbench, as
before.

**No prose.** Talk does not write or change passages. Alter and Reweave stay the only ways to change
prose.

**The conversation is not kept.** The changes are; the turns that led to them are not.

While a new tale is set up, the same rules apply to the draft, which is saved only when the writer
starts the tale.

## Considered and not chosen

- **Everything chalked**: safe, and every change costs a second press.
- **Everything applied at once, with Undo**: fastest, and Anansi would also change what nobody
  asked him to.
