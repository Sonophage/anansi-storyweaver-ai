# The premise opens the tale; threads and strings carry the rest

Seth decided this on 2026-10-28. The words are GLOSSARY.md's.

## Why

New tale asked for a premise of eight to twelve sentences covering who wants what, what stands in
the way, what it costs them and who else wants something incompatible: a story arc. The premise is
sent whole at the top of every prompt and never trimmed, so every passage was written with the arc
in view, and the model kept steering toward resolving it. Seth: "anansi with that world prompt keeps
trying to end the story."

## The decision

**The premise is one paragraph, and it is the opening**: who we are with, where and when, what they
want now and what is in their way now. Never how it ends, what it costs or what anyone will do.

**The questions the story lives on are strings**, made with the tale. Strings already reach the
model with the order not to resolve them yet.

**Everything else lives in threads**: wants and secrets in Always true and interiority, what stands
between people in relationships, backstory and how the world works in lore, places in their own
pockets. New tale makes every thread full from the start, and makes sure no two of them contradict
each other before the writer starts.

**Existing tales are left alone.** Their premises keep what they say.

## Considered and not chosen

- **The open questions inside the premise**: they would sit in the one block that is never trimmed
  and read as the next thing to answer.
- **Rewriting existing premises**: Seth chose to leave them.
